5 golf courses in Zealand that are worth every krone

~6 min read

Zealand has a handful of courses that cost like a small holiday, and fair enough, they are good. But most of the good rounds here do not cost that. Here are five courses that give you more course, more challenge and more holes for the money than the ones everyone already knows about.

Hedeland Golf Club, Copenhagen & North Zealand

Built inside Hedeland's old gravel pits and now a proper nature park, this course throws in more elevation, water and doglegs than most Zealand courses at this price. Weekday greenfee sits around 500 kr, weekend around 600 kr, and it comes with free range balls before you start. If you just want to hit balls and putt for an afternoon without booking a full round, the pay-and-play option runs about 200 kr.

Dragsholm Golf Club, West Zealand

A parkland course laid out around the 800-year-old Dragsholm Castle, with an 18-hole greenfee that moves between roughly 345 and 495 kr depending on season and time of day. The real value is the side courses: a 9-hole layout for about 250 kr all day and a par-3 course for around 125 kr, both flat-rate no matter when you show up. Bring a beginner or a rough morning after, and nobody has to sit out.

Vallø Golf Club, near Køge

27 holes split across an 18-hole championship course and a 9-hole par-3, a few minutes outside Køge. The 18 starts from around 350 kr, but the 9-hole pay-and-play is the real trick: about 150 kr for as many loops as you want in a day. A simulator bay covers the months the course does not.

Five courses, at a glance

  • Hedeland: hilly nature-park course, ~500-600 kr, free range balls.
  • Dragsholm: castle-course parkland, plus a 9-hole from 250 kr.
  • Vallø: 18 holes from 350 kr, unlimited 9-hole play for 150 kr.
  • Skjoldenæsholm: a Robert Trent Jones Jr. design from around 350 kr.
  • Frederikssund: fjord views from around 325 kr on weekdays.

Skjoldenæsholm Golf Club, Mid Zealand

Two full 18-hole courses for the price most clubs charge for one: the traditional, tree-lined Old Course, and a Robert Trent Jones Jr. design with the same strategic bunkering you would pay double for at Scandinavian. Greenfee starts around 350 kr for either, plus a 9-hole par-3 for anyone easing a guest into the game.

Frederikssund Golf Club, North Zealand

Fjord views without the North Zealand price tag. The 18-hole course runs from around 325 kr on weekdays, and the 9-hole par-3 is pay-and-play at about 125 kr on weekdays and 175 kr on weekends, walk on as many times as you like. A 24-bay range with free balls on the day you play makes it an easy one to bring a new golfer to.

A good round does not need a designer price tag.

The prices above are approximate: greenfee moves with season, day of the week and time of day, so always check the club's own price page or Golfbox before you book. And once the round is in the books, log itand post it in the group, so the rest of your group can vote on which course is next.

Common questions

Are these green fees fixed?

No. Green fees move with season, day of the week and time of day, and clubs update them through the year. Treat the numbers here as a starting point and check the club's own booking page, usually on Golfbox, before you go.

Do I need to be a member to play these courses?

No. Every course listed here takes paying guests, and most also offer a pay-and-play option on a 9-hole or par-3 course for visitors who do not want to book a full 18-hole round.

What is the cheapest way to try golf in Zealand?

Look for a club's 9-hole or par-3 pay-and-play option. Several clubs on this list charge a flat rate of 125 to 200 kr for unlimited play on their short course in a day, which is the cheapest way to bring a beginner along.
