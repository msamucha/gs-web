Golf shoes: how to choose the right pair

~5 min read

Your golf shoes are in on every single shot you hit. The driver gets maybe fourteen swings a round, the putter thirty on a rough day, but your shoes work from the first tee to the last green. Still, most golfers spend weeks comparing clubs and roughly four minutes choosing footwear. Here is how to get the only piece of gear that plays every hole right on the first try.

Buying all your gear at once? Read how to choose your first golf gear first, then come back for the shoes.

Spikes or spikeless

The first choice is traction. Spiked shoes carry soft plastic cleats that bite into the turf and hold your feet still through the swing. Spikeless shoes use a rubber sole with dozens of small nubs instead, and look close enough to trainers that you can wear them to the course, around it, and home again.

For most players, modern spikeless traction covers ninety percent of golf. Where spikes earn their keep is wet grass, sloping lies and fast swings, which in Denmark describes roughly October to April. If you only own one pair, make it spikeless for the comfort. If you play all year, the second pair should have spikes.

Waterproof is not optional in Denmark

A Danish golf season is morning dew in July and sideways rain in October, so treat waterproofing as a requirement, not a feature. Look for a proper membrane, either Gore-Tex or the brand’s own version, and check that the shoe comes with a waterproof guarantee. One or two years is standard.

Read the label carefully: water resistant means the shoe shrugs off dew for a few holes, waterproof means your socks are still dry when the rain stops. Wet feet on the fourth hole make for a very long back nine.

Fit: buy for the walk, not the look

Eighteen holes is an eight to ten kilometre walk, so buy shoes for that, not for how they photograph. Try them on late in the day when your feet are at their largest, wear the socks you actually play in, and leave a thumb’s width in front of your longest toe. The heel should sit locked in, and the sole should feel wider and flatter than a running shoe, because that base is what you swing from.

If a shoe needs breaking in, it does not fit. The pair that feels like nothing in the shop is the pair that feels like nothing on the sixteenth hole.

The short version

  • Spikeless first: your first pair, comfortable and versatile enough for most of the season.
  • Waterproof membrane: Gore-Tex or equivalent, with a guarantee. Water resistant is not the same thing.
  • Fit over looks: a thumb’s width in front of the toe, locked heel, no breaking in.
  • Two pairs beat one: rotate them and both pairs last years longer.
No one has ever putted better in wet socks.

What they should cost

Entry-level golf shoes from the big brands start around 500 to 700 kroner and are perfectly fine for your first seasons. The sweet spot sits between 800 and 1.500 kroner, which is where the waterproof membranes and the better outsoles live. Above that, you are mostly paying for the tour player on the box.

The best deal in golf is last season’s colourway: the same shoe, thirty to forty percent off, in a colour that was apparently wrong in March and is somehow fine in July.

Make them last

Golf shoes die in car boots. Take them inside, let them dry at room temperature and never on a radiator, and brush the sole off after wet rounds. If you have two pairs, alternate them so each pair dries fully between rounds. Replace soft spikes once a season, they unscrew in a minute and cost less than the balls you were going to lose anyway.

New shoes will not fix your swing

That part is on you, and if we are honest, it is mostly about the putting. What good shoes do is keep you planted and comfortable for all eighteen holes, and comfort late in the round is where scores are saved. Common questions

Do I need golf shoes as a beginner?

Not on day one. For lessons and the driving range, trainers are fine. Once you start walking full rounds, golf shoes are the first purchase that clearly pays off, before better clubs and long before a rangefinder.

Spiked or spikeless, which should I choose?

If you only buy one pair, choose spikeless for comfort and versatility. If you play through the Danish winter or swing hard, a spiked pair for the wet months is worth it.

Can I play in regular trainers?

On a dry summer course, yes. The problem is grip: grass is slippery, and your swing is built on your feet staying put. The first time you slip mid-swing in front of your friends, you will understand the price of golf shoes.

What do good golf shoes cost?

Solid entry pairs start around 500 to 700 kroner. Between 800 and 1.500 kroner you get waterproof membranes and better soles, which is where most players should land. Above that, you are paying for the logo.

When should golf shoes be replaced?

When the traction is worn smooth or they start letting water in, whichever comes first. With weekly golf that is typically every one to two seasons, twice as long if you rotate two pairs.
