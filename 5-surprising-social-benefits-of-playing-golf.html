5 Surprising Social Benefits of Playing Golf

~4 min read · By Dan Chessa, Golf Marketing Officer

Golf often gets associated with skill and scenic fairways. But beyond that there are social advantages many golfers don’t immediately think about. These are the kinds of benefits Golfsocial is all about: helping you stay connected, grow friendships, and feel part of something meaningful even when you are out there playing solo.

Here are five surprising social benefits of playing golf.

Increased sense of belonging and community

Golf isn’t just about the swing, it’s about who you share the course with. A study called The Association of Golf Participation With Health and Wellbeing: A Comparative Study in the Golf Science Journal found that golfers report significantly higher levels of social trust and personal wellbeing than the general population. Belonging comes from shared experiences: post-round cheers, discussions over swing tweaks, friendly rivalries. All of that builds community.

Stronger social networks and connections

Golf connects you with people from different generations, professions, and backgrounds. According to research in Golf & Health, the sport provides opportunities to enhance interaction across age groups and strengthen social connections. Whether you’re introducing someone new to the game, teaming up for a casual round, or following friends’ highlights, the network effect of golfing is powerful.

Improved mental wellbeing through social interaction

Multiple studies point out that playing golf helps reduce stress and improve mental health, not only because of being outdoors but strongly because of the social side. A peer-reviewed study, Beyond the Handicap: A Cross-Sectional Study of Mental Health Amongst Registered Golfers in Spain, shows that golfers over 45 had much lower rates of mental health issues than those under 45. The older golfers had a more stable relationship with golf as community and ritual, not just competition. Small talk, shared tips, laughter after a good (or bad) shot, those moments help with mood, resilience, and feeling less alone.

Even a round by yourself becomes richer when you can share it, compare it, and see how others are doing.

Boosted self-efficacy and confidence

Being part of a golf circle, seeing friends improve, getting tips, and sharing success (even little ones) contributes to stronger self-efficacy, the belief that you can improve and overcome challenges. The comparative study also found that golfers scored higher on self-efficacy around general exercise and golf participation than the wider population. Confidence on the course often carries off the course: taking on new challenges, being more outgoing, and feeling more capable.

More motivation, accountability and local impact

Even when you are playing solo, knowing there is a community watching, sharing, and engaging gives you motivation and helps you stay consistent. Surveys like Golfshake’s community study show many people stay with golf because they want to be with friends or family, to socialise, or to be part of a group. Here in Denmark there is a great example: the Women and Golf programme run by the Danish Golf Union. It’s an eight-week course designed to attract and retain female golfers through social activities and shared experiences. In 2022 it attracted 600 new female golfers across 33 clubs, and it keeps expanding. Many participants emphasised how meeting others, sharing laughs, and simply doing golf together mattered just as much as the game itself.
